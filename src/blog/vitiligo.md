Noticing a pale or white patch on your skin can raise questions. Vitiligo is one possible cause. Getting the right diagnosis helps you understand what is happening and what care choices are available.

## What is vitiligo?

Vitiligo is a condition in which areas of skin lose pigment (colour). It can affect any skin tone and may appear on different parts of the body. In some cases, hair growing in an affected area can also turn white or grey. Vitiligo is not contagious.

## How is vitiligo diagnosed?

A clinician will ask about your health and examine your skin. A dermatologist may use a special ultraviolet light to help assess the patches and distinguish vitiligo from other causes of lighter skin. Depending on your history and examination, they may suggest additional checks.

If a new patch is changing, spreading, itchy, or concerning you, arrange an assessment with a qualified doctor or dermatologist instead of relying on a photo or self-diagnosis.

## What treatment options may be discussed?

Treatment is an individual choice. A dermatologist may discuss prescription creams, light-based treatment, or camouflage products, depending on factors such as the area affected, whether patches are changing, and your preferences. Results can take time, vary between people and body areas, and may not be permanent. There is no single treatment that works for everyone.

Ask your dermatologist about expected benefits, side effects, time commitment, and what follow-up is needed. Do not start or stop prescribed treatment without discussing it with your clinician.

## Everyday skin care

- Protect lighter patches from sunburn with shade, clothing, and broad-spectrum sunscreen suitable for you.
- Avoid sunbeds and unproven skin-lightening or repigmentation products.
- If the condition affects your confidence or mood, tell your clinician. Emotional support and camouflage options may help.

## A note about homoeopathy

People may explore different approaches to support their wellbeing. Current dermatology guidance describes options such as topical medicines and phototherapy; reliable evidence that homoeopathy restores skin pigment or treats vitiligo is lacking. If you choose to consult a homoeopathic practitioner, keep your dermatologist involved and do not delay proven assessment or treatment. Tell every clinician about medicines and supplements you use.

## When to seek advice

Book an appointment with a doctor or dermatologist if you notice new or changing lighter patches, are unsure of the cause, or want to discuss treatment. A diagnosis can help rule out other conditions and make sure you have information suited to your skin and health.
